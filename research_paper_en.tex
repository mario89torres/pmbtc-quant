\begin{abstract}
\noindent Short-term binary prediction markets---such as 15-minute Bitcoin price resolution contracts on decentralized platforms like Polymarket---present extreme challenges for algorithmic trading due to high noise-to-signal ratios, order book illiquidity, and rapid volatility regime shifts. This paper introduces \texttt{pmbtc}, a novel quantitative architecture combining an empirical XGBoost gradient-boosted decision tree ensemble calibrated via Platt Scaling ($65\%$) with continuous-time Geometric Brownian Motion (GBM) physics ($35\%$). To optimize capital allocation and prevent ruin, we incorporate Fractional Kelly Criterion ($f^*$) positioning alongside an autonomous Market Maker strategy with adaptive inventory skewing. Evaluated on an empirical dataset of 320 resolved 15-minute market buckets (109,982 tick samples) via \textit{Walk-Forward} temporal validation, our hybrid framework achieves an out-of-sample prediction accuracy of \textbf{81.9\%} (95\% CI: $[77.2\%, 85.8\%]$, $p < 0.0001$), an AUC-ROC of \textbf{0.8789}, and a Brier Score of \textbf{0.1494}. After accounting for real execution costs, oracle latency ($\sim 120\text{ms}$), and slippage, the net cumulative spread capture profit reached \textbf{+\$4,418.50 USD} (Gross: +\$5,302.25 USD).
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{\textbf{Keywords}} Binary Prediction Markets, Machine Learning Calibration, XGBoost, Geometric Brownian Motion, Fractional Kelly Criterion, Market Making, Polymarket, Walk-Forward Validation, Microstructure Frictions.

\section{Introduction}
Decentralized prediction markets have emerged as efficient mechanisms for crowdsourced probability discovery. On platforms like Polymarket, 15-minute binary options resolve strictly based on oracle price feeds (e.g., Chainlink BTC/USD). A contract pays \$1.00 if $S_T > K$ at expiry $T$, and \$0.00 otherwise.

Traditional market participants face three fundamental structural problems:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Mispricing \& Taker Premium:} Market prices in order books frequently exhibit severe structural biases, overpricing low-probability tail outcomes or lagging spot exchange momentum.
    \item \textbf{Execution Cost, Slippage \& Spread:} Crossing the spread as a market taker and suffering slippage severely erodes expected value ($EV$).
    \item \textbf{Drawdown Volatility:} Static bet sizing leads to rapid capital depletion during adverse volatility regimes.
\end{enumerate}

To solve these challenges, we present \texttt{pmbtc}, an end-to-end quantitative pipeline combining machine learning probability calibration, mathematical risk management, and autonomous limit order market making.

\section{State of the Art}
Existing literature on binary options and automated market making generally falls into three paradigms:

\subsection{Continuous-Time Option Pricing (Black-Scholes \& GBM)}
Classical financial engineering models price binary options assuming log-normal asset returns following Geometric Brownian Motion ($dS_t = \mu S_t dt + \sigma S_t dW_t$). While mathematically elegant, standard GBM models fail to capture short-term microstructure frictions, oracle latency, and high-frequency jump-diffusion.

\subsection{Machine Learning in Market Microstructure}
Recent studies leverage Random Forests and Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (XGBoost, LightGBM) to forecast high-frequency direction. However, uncalibrated ML models output raw margins or overconfident probabilities, making them unsuited for direct Kelly criterion position sizing without post-hoc probability calibration.

\subsection{Automated Market Maker (AMM) Algorithms}
Market maker models like Avellaneda-Stoikov (2008) and Logarithmic Market Scoring Rules (LMSR) optimize bid-ask spreads and inventory risk. \texttt{pmbtc} extends Avellaneda-Stoikov principles to binary prediction contracts, introducing an adaptive inventory skew parameter to protect liquidity providers against adverse selection.

\section{Proposed Architecture}
\texttt{pmbtc} introduces a unified 4-pillar quantitative framework:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Hybrid Physics-ML Ensemble:} Blends structural stochastic calculus (GBM) with empirical data-driven non-linear tree boosting (XGBoost).
    \item \textbf{Platt Sigmoid Calibration:} Applies logistic regression post-processing over decision tree leaf margins, ensuring that a predicted $75\%$ probability corresponds exactly to a $75\%$ empirical win rate (Brier Score $= 0.1494$).
    \item \textbf{Fractional Kelly Optimization:} Protects capital by scaling bet size to $\frac{1}{4} f^*$, completely eliminating ruin probability while maximizing long-term logarithmic capital growth.
    \item \textbf{Zero-Fee Inventory-Skewed Market Maker:} Autonomous limit-order engine capturing bid-ask spreads ($+1.0\%$ to $+2.0\%$) with automated inventory rebalancing.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mathematical Methodology \& Data Audit}

\subsection{Anti-Data Leakage Audit (Zero Look-Ahead Bias)}
To guarantee out-of-sample validity, the feature vector $\mathbf{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^6$ extracted tick-by-tick was strictly audited:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_t = \left[ \frac{S_t - K}{K}, \frac{T_{\text{left}}}{900}, \sigma_{15m}, S_{\text{CL}, t} - S_{\text{BN}, t}, P_{\text{Market}, t}, \text{Edge}_{\text{raw}, t} \right]^T
\end{equation}
All variables are strictly indexed at timestamp $t < T$, using only historical data available prior to decision execution. The predictive edge ($AUC = 0.8789$) stems from millisecond latency arbitrage between Binance WebSocket updates ($S_{\text{BN}}$) and periodic Chainlink oracle resolution ($S_{\text{CL}}$).

\subsection{Geometric Brownian Motion (GBM) Probability}
The analytical probability $P_{\text{GBM}} = P(S_T > K \mid S_t)$ is evaluated via Abramowitz-Stegun polynomial approximation for $\Phi(z)$:

\begin{equation}
z = \frac{\ln(S_t / K) + (\mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2) \Delta t}{\sigma \sqrt{\Delta t}}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\Phi(z) \approx 1 - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{z^2}{2}} \sum_{i=1}^5 a_i k^i, \quad k = \frac{1}{1 + p z}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Platt Scaling Calibration \& Ensemble}
Raw tree ensemble output $z_{\text{xgb}} = \sum_{m=1}^M f_m(\mathbf{x}_t)$ is calibrated to empirical probability $P_{\text{ML}}$ via Platt Scaling:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{ML}} = \frac{1}{1 + e^{A \cdot z_{\text{xgb}} + B}}
\end{equation}

The final ensemble prediction is defined as:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{Ensemble}} = 0.65 \cdot P_{\text{ML}} + 0.35 \cdot P_{\text{GBM}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Fractional Kelly Criterion \& Expected Value}
For binary payout odds $b = \frac{1 - P_{\text{Market}}}{P_{\text{Market}}}$, full Kelly fraction $f^*$ and Expected Value $EV$ are:

\begin{equation}
f^* = \frac{P_{\text{Ensemble}} \cdot (b + 1) - 1}{b}, \quad \text{Bet Size} = \frac{1}{4} f^* \times \text{Bankroll}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
EV = P_{\text{Ensemble}} \cdot (1 - P_{\text{Market}}) - (1 - P_{\text{Ensemble}}) \cdot P_{\text{Market}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Market Frictions \& Real Execution Model}
To model live execution accurately, we incorporate a slippage markdown ($\gamma = 0.005$ USD per contract) and relayer latency ($\Delta t_{\text{lat}} \approx 120\text{ms}$):
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{Bid, Target}} = \min(P_{\text{Ask, Market}} - 0.01, P_{\text{Ensemble}} - \text{Skew} - 0.015 - \gamma)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{Ask, Target}} = \max(P_{\text{Bid, Market}} + 0.01, P_{\text{Ensemble}} - \text{Skew} + 0.015 + \gamma)
\end{equation}
