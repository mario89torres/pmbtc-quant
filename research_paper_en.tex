\begin{abstract}
\noindent This paper presents an honest empirical evaluation based on a custom SQLite dataset of \textbf{405 resolved market buckets (186,518 tick samples)} for 15-minute Bitcoin binary resolution contracts on Polymarket. We evaluate two algorithmic paradigms: (1) a directional taker strategy using a hybrid ensemble of Platt-calibrated XGBoost decision trees and Geometric Brownian Motion (GBM), and (2) an autonomous liquidity provision strategy (*Market Maker / Maker Player*) with dynamic inventory skewing. The empirical backtest reveals that at low edge thresholds ($\le 1.5\%$), directional signals achieve $50.4\%$ to $54.5\%$ accuracy, with a 95\% Wilson Confidence Interval crossing the break-even threshold (making low-edge signals statistically indistinguishable from random noise). However, at a strict \textbf{$3.0\%$} edge threshold, the model selects 169 high-conviction trades, achieving a \textbf{58.0\%} win rate with a 95\% CI of \textbf{$[50.5\%, 65.2\%]$}, remaining strictly above the break-even cutoff ($49.8\%$). Meanwhile, the autonomous Market Maker strategy demonstrated superior consistency, generating a Gross PnL of \textbf{+\$5,403.20 USD} and a Net PnL of \textbf{+\$4,430.62 USD} after discounting an 18\% execution friction (slippage and latency).
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{\textbf{Keywords}} Binary Prediction Markets, Real Empirical Evaluation, XGBoost, Platt Scaling, Kelly Criterion, Market Making, Wilson Confidence Intervals, Polymarket.

\section{Introduction}
Decentralized prediction markets have emerged as efficient mechanisms for crowdsourced probability discovery. On platforms like Polymarket, 15-minute binary options resolve strictly based on oracle price feeds (e.g., Chainlink BTC/USD). A contract pays \$1.00 if $S_T > K$ at expiry $T$, and \$0.00 otherwise.

Market participants face three structural challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Noise \& Taker Premium:} Crossing the spread as a taker erodes expected value if the entry edge does not exceed execution costs.
    \item \textbf{Execution Cost \& Slippage:} In high-frequency settings, slippage and relayer latency impair theoretical gains.
    \item \textbf{Ruin Risk:} Static capital allocation leads to severe drawdowns during adverse market regimes.
\end{enumerate}

We present empirical results derived from running \texttt{backtest.js} against the real dataset of 405 resolved 15-minute buckets in the \texttt{pmbtc} system.

\section{Mathematical Methodology \& Data Evaluation}

\subsection{Hybrid Ensemble \& Platt Calibration}
The model extracts the feature vector $\mathbf{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^6$ tick-by-tick:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_t = \left[ \frac{S_t - K}{K}, \frac{T_{\text{left}}}{900}, \sigma_{15m}, S_{\text{CL}, t} - S_{\text{BN}, t}, P_{\text{Market}, t}, \text{Edge}_{\text{raw}, t} \right]^T
\end{equation}

Raw tree ensemble output $z_{\text{xgb}}$ is calibrated to empirical probability $P_{\text{ML}}$ via Platt Scaling:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{ML}} = \frac{1}{1 + e^{A \cdot z_{\text{xgb}} + B}}
\end{equation}

The analytical ensemble prediction is defined as:
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{Ensemble}} = 0.65 \cdot P_{\text{ML}} + 0.35 \cdot P_{\text{GBM}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Autonomous Market Maker \& Inventory Skewing}
The autonomous Market Maker posts target limit quotes $P_{\text{Bid, Target}}$ and $P_{\text{Ask, Target}}$ adjusted by inventory skew $\text{Skew}(I)$:
\begin{equation}
\text{Skew} = \text{clamp}\left( -0.04, 0.04, \frac{\text{Inventory}}{50} \times 0.05 \right)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{Bid, Target}} = \min(P_{\text{Ask, Market}} - 0.01, P_{\text{Ensemble}} - \text{Skew} - 0.015)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
P_{\text{Ask, Target}} = \max(P_{\text{Bid, Market}} + 0.01, P_{\text{Ensemble}} - \text{Skew} + 0.015)
\end{equation}

\section{Scientific Discussion}
1. **The Truth About Low-Edge Directional Trades**: Backtest evaluation on real SQLite data confirms that filtering with low edge ($\le 1.5\%$) yields win rates of $50.4\%-54.5\%$ whose confidence intervals cross break-even thresholds. Consequently, low-edge taker signals are statistically indistinguishable from noise.
2. **High-Conviction Threshold Edge (3.0c)**: Requiring an edge $\ge 3.0\%$ isolates 169 trades where a **$58.0\%$** win rate sustains a 95\% CI of $[50.5\%, 65.2\%]$, strictly above the $49.8\%$ break-even cutoff.
3. **Consistency of Market Maker Strategy**: Capturing bid-ask spreads via limit orders with active inventory control remains the most robust mechanism to monetize Polymarket microstructure.

\section{Conclusion}
Honest empirical evaluation on `pmbtc.db` demonstrates that rather than attempting frequent directional forecasting, optimal quantitative execution favors automated liquidity provision or restricting taker entries to high-conviction thresholds ($\ge 3.0\%$).
